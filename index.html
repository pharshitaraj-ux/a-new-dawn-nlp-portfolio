Telugu Song Translation: Sadi Seyako Gaali

Translation2 stars

I translated and analysed an old Telugu song, comparing translations across Indian-language literary work. The Telugu original and full translations are in my portfolio PDF.

Meaning

The song expresses love, affection and protectiveness. The speaker gently asks the wind not to disturb the beloved, who sleeps peacefully in their lap. The beloved is called a king even without a throne or crown, showing how precious he is. Flowing water, moving leaves, moonlight and flowers create a peaceful atmosphere.

Original source

  • Film: Raja Makutam
  • Lyricist: Devulapalli Krishna Sastri
  • Music composer: Master Venu
  • Director: Bommireddy Narasimha Reddy

Comparing two translations

I compared an LLM English translation with an alternative human-style translation created for comparison. The second one is not taken from a verified published translation.

  • Cosine similarity score: not calculated
  • Lexical similarity: medium to high
  • Semantic similarity: high
  • Cultural similarity: high

Both convey the same central idea. The LLM translation stays closer to the original Telugu expressions, while the human-style version is simpler and more conversational. Poetic and cultural nuances, such as the king-without-a-throne comparison, are hard to reproduce completely in English.

Russian Broadway Maps – Moscow (Group Activity)

Group activity

Our group worked on Russian Old Moscow Map vs Modern Moscow Map: Historical Map Comparison Using NLP. We completed the discussion and activity successfully, although we could not upload it in the group.

Goal: an application that compares historical maps of Moscow with modern maps, identifies changes in streets, buildings and landmarks, and uses NLP to connect historical records with present-day locations.

How NLP is integrated

  • Extract names of streets, buildings, monuments and rivers from historical Russian documents
  • Match historical place names with modern equivalents
  • Analyse historical descriptions to understand changes in urban structure
  • Identify dates and events linked to changes in specific locations
  • Generate short explanations of differences between old and modern maps

NLP skills involved

  • Named Entity Recognition: place names, landmarks, historical figures
  • Entity Linking: historical names to modern equivalents
  • Semantic Similarity: different names or descriptions of one location
  • Information Extraction: facts from historical documents
  • Text Classification: road development, building changes, urban expansion
  • Text Summarisation: concise summaries of changes
  • Temporal Information Extraction: dates and periods

Applications

  • Historical map comparison
  • Street name identification (renamed streets)
  • Landmark tracking
  • Urban change analysis
  • Historical event mapping
  • Change explanation from historical records
  • Interactive map application

Major Project

Major Project · J Component

CrisisBridge AI

RAG-Augmented Disaster Response Prioritization System

From a flood of disaster messages to the few that need help right now.

Team: Dara Karthikeya (23MIC0008) · Sahasra Reddy (23MIC0037) · Harshita Raj (23MID0294) · Slot F1 + TF1 · November 2026

CrisisBridge AI unifies information from social media, news portals, government notifications and research databases into one prioritised format, scoring how serious each report is on a scale of 0 to 100. A commander can ask a plain question, such as which event is most urgent near a given bridge, and get a concise, cited answer.

Motivation

When flood waters rise, witnesses post about it long before an official situation report is released, while agencies such as IMD and NDMA publish alerts. Commanders then face thousands of messages mixed with rumours and irrelevant content, so the most dangerous reports are hard to find.

Large language models alone can invent places or figures, which is dangerous in a crisis. The team built around three principles:

  • Clarity: every ranking comes with reasons
  • Citing: every answer sentence points to a source report
  • Robustness: runs on a laptop CPU, and a failed source is skipped with an explanation

The Event and the Gap

During the 2015 Chennai floods, residents and volunteers used social media to ask for rescue, boats, food and medicine, and control centres had to read the requests manually.

  • Only about 0.36% of disaster-related tweets from India carry a specific geographic tag, so place must be extracted from text.
  • In one batch of 100 NDMA SACHET alerts, only 22 were in English; the rest were in Telugu, Hindi, Bengali, Odia or Marathi.

The gap: existing work either classifies messages without letting anyone ask questions, or answers from static documents rather than live incidents. CrisisBridge AI joins the two.

Architecture

Five layers in one pipeline. Each layer is a separate Python package, so it can be tested and replaced independently, and src/pipeline.py connects them.

HumAID + CrisisBenchIDRISISample (50 messages)NDMA SACHETGDACS · EONETUSGS · EMSCNews · social
1 · Ingestion layercurated_loader.py, live_sources.py, translate.py. Loads datasets, fetches 8 live sources, translates regional-language text with an LLM, de-duplicates into Report objects.
2 · NLP processing layerpreprocess.py, classifier.py, ner.py, urgency.py. TF-IDF + logistic regression (10 classes), rules + IDRISI gazetteer NER, urgency score 0–100.
3 · Indexing layerembeddings.py, indexer.py. all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings (384-d) in FAISS IndexFlatIP, with TF-IDF + SVD / NumPy fallback.
4 · RAG retrieval and generationretriever.py, generator.py. Search re-ranked by location, urgency, recency and need; Groq or Gemini writes a cited answer, with an extractive fallback.
5 · Coordinator interfaceapi.py + web/, streamlit_app.py, cli.py. FastAPI web console (with original-language toggle), Streamlit console and command-line tool.

Each live source reports OK or SKIP with a reason, so a failing source never stops the pipeline.

NLP Pipeline

Every message, from a dataset, a live feed or the console, follows the same path.

Raw messagepost, tweet or alert
Translateonly if not English (LLM)
Preprocessstrip URLs, RT, @user; split hashtags
ClassifyTF-IDF + LogReg, 10 classes
Extractlocation, people count, need types
Score urgencyadditive 0–100 with reasons
Reportpriority band, added to index
  • Language check and translation: text mostly in non-Latin scripts such as Tamil, Devanagari and Telugu is translated into English.
  • Preprocessing: links, HTML entities, retweet prefixes and @mentions are removed; camel-case hashtags are split (#EcuadorEarthquake becomes “Ecuador Earthquake”); case is kept for the entity extractor.
  • Classification: TF-IDF features (1–2 word n-grams and 3–5 character n-grams) feed a multinomial logistic regression (C = 4, balanced class weights), trained on HumAID plus CrisisBench tweets, giving probabilities for 10 humanitarian classes.
  • Entity extraction: locations come from prepositional phrases and landmarks (such as “near the XYZ bridge”) plus a gazetteer of 683 place names learned from IDRISI; people counts come from phrases such as “family of 5”; 9 need types are detected.
  • Urgency scoring: an additive 0–100 score from category (0–35, from class probabilities), life threat (+25, capped at 35 in total), explicit urgency (+6, capped at 12), people affected (+5·log2(1+n), capped at 15) and primary need (0–15), reduced by 25 for resolved cases. Bands: critical ≥ 70, high ≥ 45, medium ≥ 25, low < 25.
  • Embedding and retrieval: reports are embedded with all-MiniLM-L6-v2 and searched with FAISS (top 100). Results are re-ranked with 0.55·cosine + 0.20·location + 0.15·urgency + 0.10·recency (+0.25·need when raised), and the top five go to the LLM.
  • Generation: the top five reports are passed to the LLM with a prompt that says to answer only from the numbered reports and cite each sentence. If there is no API key or the call fails, an extractive answer is built from the same reports.
“4 people trapped on the second floor near XYZ bridge, need rescue”
→ Location: XYZ bridge | Need: Rescue | People: 4 | Urgency 77.0/100 = Critical

Documented output example (Figure 4 of the report): a Tamil message, translated to “A family of 5 is trapped on the roof of their house due to flooding in Velachery; a boat is needed.”, was extracted as Location: Velachery, Need: Rescue, People: 5, and scored 93.3 / 100 (Critical).

Users, Inputs and Outputs

Users

  • Incident commander: asks plain-language questions, reads cited answers, works from the priority feed
  • Field volunteer / NGO worker: pastes a message in any Indian language to get place, need, people and priority
  • Analyst / researcher: exports the ranked feed as CSV; compares sources; retrains models
  • Administrator: sets LLM keys in .env, chooses sources, rebuilds the index

Inputs

  • Commander question (English, up to 500 characters)
  • New message in English or an Indian language (up to 2,000 characters)
  • Live alerts, headlines and social posts (RSS/Atom, JSON)
  • Labelled training and evaluation data
  • Configuration through a .env file, never shown in the interface

Outputs

  • Structured report: category, locations, people count, needs, urgency score, band and reasons
  • One-line summary, e.g. Location | Need | People | Priority
  • Cited answer of up to six sentences plus the five source reports
  • Priority feed with filters and CSV export
